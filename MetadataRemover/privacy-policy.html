Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Thank you for using MetadataRemover. We built this app with privacy as our core principle.

Our Promise: Your photos never leave your device. All metadata reading and removal happens entirely on your iPhone, iPad, Mac or Android device. We never receive, see, or store your photos or videos, their metadata, or anything written in them.

Information We Collect

We never collect your photos, videos, or their metadata (locations, names, dates, camera details, captions, AI prompts, or file names).

The app sends usage statistics and crash reports (you can turn both off), and purchase information to the service that keeps Premium active. An account is optional: the app works fully without one. If you choose to sign in, your account ID, e-mail address and (for Google accounts) name are stored so Premium can follow you to your other devices. The app does not show ads and does not track you across other apps or websites.

Photo and File Processing

All photo and video metadata reading and removal is performed locally on your device: on Apple devices with Apple's native frameworks (ImageIO and AVFoundation), on Android with the app's own file reader. Your photos are processed in memory or in temporary files that the app deletes (on Android, copies you shared are kept for up to 24 hours, then deleted), and are never transmitted over the internet by the app.

On iPad, Mac and Android, files you open or drop into the app are read on your device. Cleaned copies are saved as new files where you choose; originals are never changed. On the Mac, originals are moved to the Trash only if you turn that on.

Photo Library and Gallery Access

The app requests permission to access your photo library or gallery. This access is used to:

  • Read photos and videos you select for metadata viewing, removal, or editing
  • Save cleaned copies back to your library (when you choose to save)
  • Delete originals, only when you ask (the system always asks you to confirm)
  • Scan your library for photos that reveal private information, if you use Scan My Library. The scan runs on your device and its results are stored only on your device.

Android: photos you open come through the system file browser, the share menu or "Open with" and need no permission. Access to photos and videos (and "media location" access, so Android shows the real place a photo was taken) is asked for only when you run Scan My Library, and on Android 17 when you first open a photo. You can allow all photos, only selected photos, or none; every other feature works without it.

You can change or revoke this permission at any time: on iPhone and iPad in Settings > Privacy & Security > Photos > MetadataRemover; on the Mac in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Photos; on Android in Settings > Apps > Metadata Remover > Permissions > Photos and videos.

Place Names and Maps

When a photo contains a location, the app can show a nearby place name (and on Apple devices a map). To do this, the photo's coordinates are sent to the device's map service: Apple Maps on Apple devices (a service provided by Apple under Apple's privacy policy), and on Android the device's geocoding service (on most devices provided by Google, under Google's privacy policy). When you search for a place while editing a photo's location, your search text is sent to the same service. No photo or other metadata is sent. On Android, "Open in Maps" hands the coordinates to the maps app you choose. Without an internet connection, the app works without place names.

Usage Analytics (iPhone, iPad and Android)

To understand which features are used and how the subscription offer performs, the app on iPhone, iPad and Android sends usage events to Google Analytics for Firebase (Google LLC), for example "a photo was cleaned", "the library scan finished", or "the subscription screen was shown". The Mac app sends no usage analytics. These events:

  • Never include your photos, their contents, or any metadata values
  • Carry a random app-instance identifier created by Firebase, not your name, e-mail, Apple Account, Google Account or account ID
  • Are not used for advertising or to track you across other apps and websites. The app does not use the advertising identifier (on Android the advertising ID permission is removed) and never asks to track you.
  • Include basic information Firebase collects automatically, such as device model, system version, app version, and language. Google derives an approximate country or region from the connection; the app never reads your device's location.

Analytics data is kept for 14 months. Google's handling of this data is described at firebase.google.com/support/privacy and policies.google.com/privacy.

Crash Reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, a technical report (device model, system version, app version, and where in the app it happened) is sent to Firebase Crashlytics (Google LLC) so we can fix it. This applies to iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android. Crash reports never contain your photos, their metadata, file names, your e-mail or your account ID, and are kept for 90 days.

Turning Usage Data Off

Usage analytics and crash reports follow one switch, Share Anonymous Usage Data, which you can turn off at any time:

  • iPhone and iPad: Settings > Apps > MetadataRemover > Share Anonymous Usage Data (on iPad also in the app's Settings)
  • Mac: MetadataRemover > Settings > Privacy
  • Android: in the app, Settings (the gear on Home; on tablets, Settings in the side rail) > Share Anonymous Usage Data

When it is off, the app sends no usage events and no crash reports, and it does not pass the analytics details described under "Subscriptions and Purchases" to RevenueCat.

Subscriptions and Purchases

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, purchases are made with Apple's In-App Purchase through the App Store; on Android, with Google Play Billing through Google Play. We do not have access to your payment information. You can manage or cancel subscriptions in your Apple Account settings (Apple) or in Google Play > Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions (Android).

To know whether Premium is active and to keep it on your devices, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.), which processes purchase information on our behalf. RevenueCat receives:

  • Your purchase history in this app (the App Store or Google Play transaction details: product, dates, renewal status, store country)
  • An app user ID: a random ID, or your account ID if you signed in
  • Basic device details (model, system version, app version)
  • If Share Anonymous Usage Data is on: three short labels (which screen opened the subscription offer, the app's language, and whether the app came from the App Store, Google Play or a test build) and, on iPhone, iPad and Android, the Firebase app-instance identifier, so that purchase events (such as a renewal) can be added to our Google Analytics reports

RevenueCat never receives your photos or their metadata. It is used only to verify purchases, keep Premium on your devices, and for sales reports. See the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

Optional Account

You can sign in with Apple or Google so Premium follows you to your other devices: on iPhone and Android phones from Home > "Sync Premium Across Devices", on iPad, Mac and Android from Settings > Account, or from the subscription screen. On Android, Google sign-in uses Android's Credential Manager. It is optional; the app works fully without an account. Sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication (Google LLC), which stores:

  • Your account ID
  • Your e-mail address (with Sign in with Apple you can hide it behind Apple's relay address; the app asks Apple for nothing else)
  • For Google accounts, the name and profile picture address Google provides

We use them only for signing in and for syncing Premium. Your photos, their metadata and your settings are never stored in the account.

What Is Linked to You

  • Without an account, nothing the app sends is tied to your name or e-mail. Purchases are recorded under a random ID.
  • When you are signed in, your e-mail, name (Google) and account ID identify you, and your RevenueCat record uses your account ID. Your purchase history, the labels described above and the Firebase app-instance identifier are then held on a record that is linked to your account.
  • Usage events in Google Analytics and crash reports are never sent with your account ID, e-mail or name.

Deleting Your Account and Your Data

  • In the app: open the account screen (iPhone and Android phones: the account row on Home; iPad, Mac and Android: Settings > Account) and choose Delete Account. Your account (account ID, e-mail and name) is deleted from Firebase Authentication. For Sign in with Apple, the permission you gave MetadataRemover is revoked as well.
  • Purchase record: the record RevenueCat holds for your account (purchase history and the labels described above) is not deleted automatically when you delete your account, so your purchase stays restorable and our sales records stay complete. To have it deleted, e-mail com.loop.to.infinity@gmail.com from the address of your account, or tell us the account you used. We delete it and confirm within 30 days.
  • If you can't open the app, e-mail the same address and we delete the account for you. Without an account nothing is held under your name or e-mail.
  • Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription and does not refund a purchase. Your purchase stays restorable with Restore Purchases on the Apple Account or Google Play account that made it; cancel a subscription in your Apple Account settings or in Google Play > Payments & subscriptions. Apple and Google keep their own purchase records under their privacy policies.
  • Usage data is not tied to your account. Turn the switch off to stop it; analytics data expires after 14 months and crash reports after 90 days.

Data Stored on Your Device

The app stores the following locally on your device:

  • Usage counter: Tracks daily free tier usage (resets daily)
  • Premium status: Whether Premium is active (from Apple's StoreKit or Google Play Billing, and RevenueCat)
  • Preferences: Your "What to remove" choices, saved presets, and app settings; on the Mac, the folder you chose for saving and the file-name suffix
  • Library scan results: Which photos reveal private information, so the scan doesn't start over

This data stays on your device, is shared only between the app and its own extensions (the Share extension and, on the Mac, the Finder Quick Action), and is deleted when you uninstall the app. On Android, if you use Android's backup, your "What to remove" choices and the usage-data switch are included in your own Google backup; the daily count, scan results, sign-in and purchase data are not.

Share Extension, Share Menu, Finder Quick Action and Shortcuts

Cleaning photos from the Share sheet, the Mac's Share menu, the Finder Quick Action, with Shortcuts, or from Android's share menu and "Open with" works the same way: on your device, without uploading anything. On iPhone and iPad these features record usage events on your device, which the app sends the next time you open it (subject to the switch described above).

Third-Party Services

  • Apple: App Store and In-App Purchase, PhotoKit (photo access, with your permission), Apple Maps (place names and place search), Sign in with Apple
  • Google: on Android, Google Play and Google Play Billing, the device's geocoding service (place names and place search), Credential Manager for Google sign-in
  • Google Analytics for Firebase (Google LLC): Usage analytics, iPhone, iPad and Android
  • Firebase Crashlytics (Google LLC): Crash reports
  • Firebase Authentication and Google Sign-In (Google LLC): Only if you sign in
  • RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.): Purchase verification and Premium status

We do not use advertising networks, and we do not sell your data or share it with data brokers.

Children's Privacy

The app is rated 4+ on the App Store and for everyone on Google Play, and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13; an account is optional and never needed to use the app.

Data Security

Your photos and their metadata are never transmitted by the app, and we run no servers of our own. What the app does send (described above) goes over encrypted connections to the services named in this policy.
